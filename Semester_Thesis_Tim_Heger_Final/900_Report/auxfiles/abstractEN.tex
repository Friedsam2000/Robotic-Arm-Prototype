This thesis continues the development of a lightweight robotic arm, building on the master thesis of Samuel Zeitler.
The existing prototype is enhanced with a focus on improving robustness and durability while maintaining as much of the
lightweight structure as possible. The mechanical design is revised to include ball bearings and aluminum axes among other improvements,
and a planetary gearbox is added to the yaw joint to increase the available torque by a factor of 8.
Furthermore, a modular end-effector design is introduced, featuruing a two-fingered gripper.
The MATLAB-based software suite is extended with more advanced control algorithms, utilizing the indirect
torque measurement of the servo motors. This improved design is assembled to create a reliable and modular experimental
platform for the exploration of robotic arm control strategies at the Chair of Automatic Control Theory.
